# Research desks — News & macro, Market mood

The Fundamentals desk's checklist lives in SKILL.md. This file covers the other two desks. Each desk reports only what its sources support; whatever a desk couldn't find stays out of the rest of the answer.

## 1. News & macro desk

### What to collect
- **Company:** the last two results (headline numbers and what management said), large orders or contracts, capex and expansion plans, acquisitions and divestments, management or auditor changes, regulatory actions, litigation, credit-rating changes from CRISIL / ICRA / CARE / India Ratings (credit ratings on the company's debt are fine to report — they aren't stock tips).
- **Sector:** demand trend, pricing power, competitive moves, policy changes.
- **Macro — only what touches this business.** A starting map, not a checklist:

| Business type | Macro that usually matters |
| --- | --- |
| Banks, NBFCs, housing finance, real estate, autos | RBI repo rate and policy stance, credit growth, CPI inflation |
| IT services, pharma and chemical exporters | INR/USD, US and European demand; US FDA actions for pharma |
| Paints, tyres, aviation, oil marketing, chemicals | Crude oil and other input prices |
| Cement, steel, metals, capital goods, infra | Government capex in the Union Budget, commodity prices, import competition |
| FMCG, consumer durables | Rural demand, monsoon, food inflation |
| Power, renewables, defence, electronics | Policy (PLI, tariffs, auctions), order pipelines |

Ground each macro line in a sourced figure ("repo rate X% — RBI"). A macro sentence with no number and no source is filler; drop it.

### Events vs opinions
- An **event** is something that happened: results declared, an order won, a rate changed, a plant commissioned. Events carry weight.
- An **opinion** is someone's view: a columnist, a fund manager interview, a forum post. Use opinions only as "some analysts argue…", lightly, and never relay brokerage ratings or target prices.

### Recency
Focus on roughly the last three months for company news. Older items only if they still shape the story (a pending regulatory case, a large ongoing capex).

### Sources
Company filings and announcements (via Screener's Documents section or the company's investor page), Moneycontrol, Economic Times, Business Standard, Mint; RBI, MOSPI and PIB for macro figures.

### Output
- Quick modes: 1–3 one-line items, each with its source.
- Deep Dive: table rows — What happened · When (period, e.g. "Q2 FY27 results", "last month") · Why it matters for a long-term holder · Source.

## 2. Market mood desk

Used in Deep Dive and Bull vs Bear. It answers "how does the market currently feel about this stock?" — useful context for why a valuation is where it is, and a guard against reading only one side of the story.

### Inputs, heaviest first
1. **News tone** — headlines about the company over the last 4–6 weeks: mostly positive, mostly negative, or mixed? Look at the spread; one dramatic story is not a trend.
2. **Institutional flows** — change in FII and DII holding over the last 2–4 quarters (from the shareholding pattern), plus bulk and block deals.
3. **Insider activity** — promoter and insider buys or sells from SAST / insider-trading disclosures. Promoters buying with their own money is notable. Selling can have ordinary reasons (tax, estate planning, an offer-for-sale to meet minimum public shareholding) — say so when the reason is stated.
4. **Investor forums, lightest weight** — ValuePickr threads, r/IndiaInvestments, r/IndianStockMarket. These are opinions, often from small samples, and crowd excitement can run ahead of the facts. Use them only to spot recurring themes or worries, never as evidence.

### Band and confidence
- **Band:** Positive / Mildly positive / Neutral / Mixed / Mildly negative / Negative. Use **Mixed** when inputs point in clearly different directions; **Neutral** only when the inputs are genuinely quiet.
- **Confidence:** High when three or more inputs agree and the data is reasonably full · Medium when inputs partly agree · Low when inputs are thin, contradictory, or mostly forum chatter. Say why.

### Divergences are the most useful part
Name them plainly: "Headlines upbeat after the order win, but FIIs have cut holding for four straight quarters"; "Promoters bought shares while news tone stayed negative". A divergence isn't a prediction — it's a question the user may want answered.

### Never
- Treat mood as a forecast ("sentiment is bullish so the stock will rise").
- Let mood change the fundamental-quality verdict.
- Report brokerage ratings, target prices, or "experts say buy".

### Output
Band + confidence, then 2–4 lines: the dominant narrative, the key divergence (if any), and the data limits.
