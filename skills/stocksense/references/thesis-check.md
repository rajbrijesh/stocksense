# Thesis Check — revisiting an earlier view

A long-term view is a set of claims about a business: margins will hold, debt will keep falling, the new plant will fill up. New results either support those claims or they don't. Checking them one by one is more useful than starting fresh — and it keeps a single noisy quarter from rewriting the whole story.

## Step 1 — Find the earlier view

In this order:
1. **Past conversations.** If a tool to search earlier conversations is available, search with the company name or ticker plus words like "Deep Dive", "checkpoints", "watch-points". Take from it: the quality verdict, the watch-points, the checkpoints or "thing to track", and the key figures with their periods.
2. **What the user gives you.** If they paste or describe their earlier notes, use those.
3. **Nothing found.** Ask one short question: "What were you watching — or shall I set fresh checkpoints?" If they don't have any, do a Quick Take and end it with three measurable checkpoints for next time.

The earlier view was an analysis, not the user's decision — don't write "you decided to buy". If the earlier view came from your own past answer, call it "the earlier view" or "the last analysis".

## Step 2 — Fetch the latest results

Fundamentals desk: the latest quarter and TTM figures for every checkpoint metric. News & macro desk: what has happened since the earlier view. Keep periods explicit — the comparison is meaningless otherwise.

## Step 3 — The checkpoint table

| Checkpoint | Then (period) | Now (period) | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Status** is one of: *Confirmed* · *Weakened* · *Too early to tell*.

Add any new issue that wasn't on the list as its own row, marked **New** ("Promoter pledge rose to 12% — new since the last view").

## Step 4 — Has the overall picture changed?

- Restate fundamental quality — unchanged, improved or weakened — with the one or two facts behind it.
- **Be honest about the time window.** A multi-year thesis is rarely proven or disproven by one quarter. Check for seasonality and one-offs (exceptional items, tax write-backs, inventory gains) before calling something a trend. When it's one quarter in, say "too early to tell" rather than forcing a status.
- **Price is context, not the scorecard.** You may mention how the share price moved since the earlier view, and against the Nifty 50, but the verdict on the thesis rests on business facts. A stock can fall while the thesis holds, and rise while it weakens.

## Step 5 — One lesson, and the next checkpoints

- **One concrete lesson** for the next review, in 2–4 sentences of plain prose — something specific enough to change what gets watched. E.g. "Margins proved more sensitive to crude than the earlier view assumed; track crude alongside OPM."
- **Updated checkpoints** for the next quarter: metric, where it is now, what would shift the picture.
- Closing disclaimer.

## Never

- Call the thesis "right" or "wrong" as if it were a trade result.
- Suggest buying more, averaging down, booking profit or exiting.
