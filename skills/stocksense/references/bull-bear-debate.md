# Bull vs Bear — debate protocol, and the three investor lenses

A one-sided summary tends to miss the question a sceptic would ask. Making two researchers argue — and then checking every disputed claim against the numbers — shows the user which arguments actually hold up, which is more useful than a list of pros and cons. The debate is about the **business and its valuation**, never about whether to buy or sell.

## 1. Ground rules

- **Evidence only from the desks.** Both researchers use only facts the research desks found, with their sources. No new numbers, no "industry experts say", no price predictions or targets. If a side needs a figure the desks didn't find, it says the figure is unavailable.
- **Steelman both sides.** The bull is not a cheerleader and the bear is not a doomsayer; each makes the strongest honest case and concedes what the data plainly shows.
- **Engage, don't list.** Rebuttals answer the other side's specific point ("Bear says margins fell three points — true, but two of those came from a one-off inventory write-down") instead of repeating a list.
- **No verdict on the stock.** Neither side argues "buy" or "sell". They argue whether the business is strong or weak, improving or deteriorating, cheap or expensive for what it is.

## 2. Structure

1. **Bull opening** — 3–4 points from: growth runway, moat and competitive advantages, financial strength, positive recent developments. Each point is claim + number + source.
2. **Bear opening** — 3–4 points from: risks and challenges, competitive weaknesses, negative indicators, valuation stretch, governance or macro threats. Same evidence standard. Where the bear contradicts a bull point directly, say so.
3. **Rebuttals, round 1** — the Bull answers the Bear's strongest point; the Bear answers the Bull's strongest point. Two or three lines each.
4. **Round 2** — only when the user asks for a deeper or longer debate: one more rebuttal each, on the points still contested.
5. **Moderator's scorecard** (section 3).
6. **What would settle it** — 2–3 measurable checkpoints: the metric, where it is now (with period), and what level or direction would shift the picture. E.g. "EBITDA margin back above 20% (now 17.4%, Q2 FY27)".
7. Closing disclaimer.

## 3. The moderator's scorecard

| Point in dispute | Bull says | Bear says | What the data shows | Better supported |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Better supported** is one of: *Bull* · *Bear* · *Unresolved*.

How the moderator judges:
- Each point on its evidence — not on which side spoke first or last, or which sounded more confident.
- **Unresolved** is honest when the data genuinely can't decide (it hinges on a future event, or the figure is unavailable). But don't mark everything unresolved to look balanced: if the numbers clearly back one side on a point, say so.
- **No overall winner.** Don't total the column ("Bull 3, Bear 2") — a tally is a verdict in disguise. The scorecard tells the user which claims hold up; weighing them is the user's job.

## 4. Length and the Deep Dive tab

- **Mode 6 in chat:** about 400–600 words; openings as short bullet points, rebuttals as short paragraphs, the scorecard as a table.
- **Deep Dive tab:** openings of three points each, one rebuttal each, a scorecard of 3–5 rows, and the "what would settle it" checkpoints (which also feed the View tab's checkpoints).

## 5. The three investor lenses

The same facts read differently depending on temperament. Describe how this stock looks through three lenses — never which lens the user should adopt, and never an action.

- **Growth-seeking** — cares about runway, reinvestment rate and operating leverage; will tolerate volatility and a rich valuation if growth holds. Asks: is there a long growth path, and is it funded sensibly?
- **Balanced** — wants quality at a reasonable price; weighs growth against valuation and balance-sheet strength.
- **Capital-preservation** — prioritises balance-sheet strength, cash flow, dividends, governance and low leverage; worries about drawdowns, pledging, cyclicality and concentration.

Format, for each lens: one line **finds appealing:** … and one line **would worry about:** … — each tied to a number from the research.
